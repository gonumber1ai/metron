## Day 1 — the 30-second warning

**Subject:** The 30 seconds before
**Preview:** Try this tonight. It costs nothing and it will tell you something.

> Your body warns you.
>
> About thirty seconds before the end, it sends a signal. Small, specific, and completely reliable. It has been doing this your entire life.
>
> You have almost certainly never noticed it. That is why the finish seems to arrive out of nowhere — not because you are broken, but because nobody has ever told you there was something to look for.
>
> Here's a test. Costs nothing, takes one session, and you can do it tonight.
>
> On your own, sometime in the next day or two, try to answer one question while it's happening: **on a scale of 1 to 10, where am I right now?**

> That's it. Don't try to last. Don't change anything. Just try to put a number on it, moment to moment.
>
> Almost every man who tries this finds the same thing: he can name 3, and he can name 9, and there is nothing in between. He goes from "fine" to "too late" with no middle.

## Day 2 — the anti-pill argument

**Subject:** Worth knowing
**Preview:** About the "natural" ones especially.

> Not a moral argument. A mechanical one.
>
> A pill works **on** you. Training works **in** you. Every night that goes well with a pill teaches you that you need one — and that belief does real damage, because the fear of finishing fast is itself one of the causes of finishing fast. You end up with the original problem plus new evidence for the thing underneath it.
>
> Sprays have a second cost that's harder to see. They reduce sensation — which sounds helpful and isn't. Feeling exactly where you are is the one skill that fixes this. Numb yourself and you get worse at reading yourself, not better. When the spray runs out you're not back at the start, you're behind it.
>
> Then there's the category sold as natural. Health regulators around the world keep finding undeclared pharmaceutical drugs inside supplements marketed as herbal — the same compounds as prescription pills, at a dose nobody measured, in a capsule nobody labelled. If you've been taking those, you may already be taking the thing you were trying to avoid.
>
> None of it teaches your body anything. The day you stop, you're unchanged.
>
> **[The alternative takes twelve days →]**
>
> *If a doctor prescribed you something, don't stop on your own. Talk to whoever prescribed it.*

## Day 5 — the objection

**Subject:** If you've already tried exercises
**Preview:** Almost everyone is doing half of one.

> This comes up constantly, and the answer is more interesting than it sounds.
>
> Nearly every article written about this tells men to do a particular exercise. Nearly every one describes **half** of it.
>
> There are two halves. One is the half everybody knows and everybody does. The other is the half that actually governs control — and it's almost never explained, which is why men do it for months and feel nothing.
>
> There's a worse version of this. A meaningful share of men who finish fast are already **too tight** down there, not too weak. For those men, the exercise everyone recommends makes the problem harder to solve, not easier. Every rep is a step backwards.

> If you get aching, discomfort after sitting a long time, or a permanently clenched feeling — that's likely you.
>
> Day 3 of the programme works out which of the two you are, and gives you a different protocol depending on the answer. If you're the tight kind, you stop the exercise entirely and do something else instead.
